# Making PINNs Work

**A practical course on why physics-informed neural networks fail to converge — and what to do about it.**

Prof. Dr. Dmitry Mikhaylov · Abu Dhabi Maritime Academy (AD Ports Group) · Kyrgyz National University

[![ORCID](https://img.shields.io/badge/ORCID-0009--0009--2108--6820-a6ce39)](https://orcid.org/0009-0009-2108-6820)

---

Most material on physics-informed neural networks teaches the happy path: here is the
equation, here is the loss, here is the solution. Then you write your first PINN, the loss
does not go down — or worse, it goes down and the answer is still wrong — and you are on
your own.

The methods that fix this exist and are well published. They are scattered across papers
and rarely taught together. This course collects them.

Every module is a runnable notebook on an open benchmark problem, in plain PyTorch, with
no PINN library between you and the mathematics. Each one is built around a specific way
PINNs fail, how to diagnose it, and what to do.

![Naive versus balanced loss weighting on the Helmholtz benchmark](02_loss_balancing.png)

*Module 02. Two runs of the same network on the same problem with the same budget. The
orange one has the **lower** equation loss and a thirty-three times larger error - it does
not even respect the boundary condition. Judging a PINN by its training curve is how you
ship that model.*

## Prerequisites

You can train an ordinary neural network in PyTorch and you know what a partial
differential equation is. No computational physics background needed. A willingness to
debug something that is not converging is essential.

## The two ideas the course is built on

**Module 00 talks you out of it.** A large share of PINN failures are problems that should
never have been given to a PINN. If a well-posed forward problem has a working solver, a
PINN will usually be slower and less accurate. PINNs earn their place on inverse problems,
sparse and noisy data, and settings where you need a differentiable, fast surrogate.

**Module 02 shows that the loss is not the error.** On the Helmholtz benchmark, the naive
run reaches a *lower* equation loss than the corrected one and a **thirty-three times
larger** error against the exact solution. Anyone judging by the training curve would pick
the wrong model. Module 08 is about what to watch instead.

## Running the notebooks

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt

```

Everything runs on a laptop CPU. Module 01 takes about two minutes, module 02 about
ninety seconds per run.
